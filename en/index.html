Segurança Social DiretaInstituto de Informática

Requests about the work contracts of employees

Social Security is a system created by the government to protect people in situations like illness, unemployment, old age or maternity. It gives financial support and access to essential services. It is also the platform where employers keep their legal obligations up to date.

Challenges

  • 01Adapt the feature and the work routine to the new Simplified Contribution Cycle in the daily life of companies
  • 02Give more autonomy to manage work contracts and to make requests about them
  • 03Reduce bureaucracy and give more context to external and internal users
  • 04Automate and reduce the time to handle requests to change work contracts.
  • 05Avoid errors in workers' information
  • 06Make sure the contract information of employees is more accurate, before the move to the feature that calculates the contribution obligations of companies

Results

+60Krequests solved

Employers now have the autonomy to ask for changes to their workers' contracts, without opening support tickets.

55%faster

After the launch, we analysed internal data. We saw a drop in the average time that Social Security needed to handle the requests.

Process overview

Four steps, from alignment to validation

Step 1

Aligning requirement priorities

What we did
We held sessions to align and prioritise requirements with the project team (Instituto de Informática) and the business (Social Security).
Deliverables
A map of user needs and business needs.

Main learnings

  • Priorities and a process that was not clearCompanies had a lot of extra work, and they did not know how long a request could take. They could not follow the status of the request, so some companies checked their message inbox every day to see if there was an answer.
  • Sending requests and documentsThe process had problems with documents: incomplete requests or missing documents, scans with bad quality, too much paper, and long documents where only a few fields were useful to identify the worker.
  • Time to handle a requestFrom the moment the company starts the special registration until the request is approved, it can take one month.

Step 2

Ideation and refining the solution

What we did
We held ideation sessions with the project team to simplify the planned flowchart.
Deliverables
A flowchart of the new solution and prototype flows for the company profile and for the Social Security employee profile.

Company needs

  • A clear process that they can followA map of the existing requests, with the details of the worker's contract. An area only for requests to change contracts, where the company can follow them with more autonomy and does not depend only on the messages area with electronic notifications.

Business needs

  • A work area only for the contract requests that companies sendMoving the handling of contract requests directly into the Segurança Social Direta portal. Automatic checks for simpler requests. More control and more trust in the request information, because companies send requests from the worker's contract page.

Step 3

Design system, accessibility and content alignment with the usability team of Instituto de Informática

What we did
We held review sessions about the design system, accessibility and content of the proposed flows.
Deliverables
Content improvements, a review of how the components are used, and a map of the accessibility documentation.

Step 4

Validation research

What we did
We ran usability tests for each type of request, from the company's point of view, to validate our solutions.
Deliverables
One-page reports with a map of opportunities and improvements for the solutions. They also helped to prioritise the project roadmap.

Segurança Social DiretaInstituto de Informática

Simplifying the Contribution Cycle

Social Security is a system created by the government to protect people in situations like illness, unemployment, old age or maternity. It gives financial support and access to essential services. It is also the platform where employers register and meet their obligations.

Challenge

  • 01Automate the calculation of contributions to remove repetitive manual tasks.
  • 02Get more precise and reliable data to manage contributions.
  • 03Reduce the workload to prepare and send the monthly declarations to Social Security.
  • 04Transparency in the calculation of contribution obligations.
  • 05A simpler way to send late communications and value corrections.

Results

11 → 20From 11 days to 20 days

Employers now have more days to get organised and to correct or add a worker's salary, so they can meet their contribution obligations.

100%automated

Employers no longer need 10 days to send their workers' salary data to Social Security. Social Security organises this data and calculates the amounts automatically. The employer only needs to confirm the values and create the payment receipt.

Process overview

Five steps, from discovery to validation

Step 1

Discovery research

What we did
We held in-depth interviews, with interview guides and observation, with companies and with Social Security employees.
Deliverables
A report that explains how companies, employees and services take part in managing the contribution calculation and in creating the contribution payment receipt.

Main learnings

  • The Social Security employee's viewThe processes are manual and slow, because employees handle the Salary Declarations (DR) that companies send every month. One company can send 30 to 60 DR files, and some are still sent on paper. Also, fixing errors is a manual task, and it creates a dependency on these features inside Social Security.
  • The taxpayer's viewThere is pressure on accountants, because companies prefer to give the DR work to them. This is a big responsibility when errors appear in the company's current account at Social Security. People also said they were afraid of corrections, because they need to send two files: 1 file with the salaries that were already sent, and 1 file with the correct salary data for the workers.

Step 2

Workshop with project stakeholders

What we did
We held workshop sessions to build the solution flow and to prioritise with MoSCoW. This helped to map the steps to build the solution in the project.
Deliverables
A future journey for the solution and a priority map for the project roadmap.

Company needs

  • A faster and more transparent processA feature that organises the salaries and the calculation of the workers' contribution obligations for payment. It makes it easier to correct or validate salaries, without creating and sending duplicate files to Social Security. Companies have autonomy over changes to their workers' salaries.

Business needs

  • Automatic calculation and handling of salariesSalaries and contributions are calculated automatically, with the information from the workers' contracts, and with automatic salary ranges and work breaks. Salaries are validated automatically. People only handle salaries by hand in specific situations, in other words, in exception cases.

Step 3

Ideation and refining the solution

What we did
We held ideation sessions with the project team to simplify the planned flowchart.
Deliverables
Prototype flows for the company profile and for the Social Security employee profile.

Step 4

Design system, accessibility and content alignment with the usability team of Instituto de Informática

What we did
We held review sessions about the design system, accessibility and content of the flow prototypes.
Deliverables
Content improvements, a review of how the components are used, new components, and a map of the accessibility documentation.

Step 5

Validation research

What we did
We ran usability tests for each step of the contribution calculation (confirmation, contribution obligation, value management, value communication requests and communications), from the company's point of view, to validate our solutions.
Deliverables
One-page reports with a map of opportunities and improvements for the solutions. They helped to prioritise the project roadmap.
